# V7 phase 1a: the asset library and realistic people

This is the first part of replacing the game's code-built models with professionally made, realistic ones. This part adds the asset pipeline, the asset registry, the runtime loader and every person in the game. Vehicles and houses come next, from the Sketchfab models on the download list.

All gameplay is unchanged:

- Collision boxes, physics bodies, routes, NPC behaviour and interactions are the same as before.
- Only what's drawn changes, and the old figures still exist. They show while a model loads, and everywhere with **`?classic`** in the address.

## People

- **41 realistic characters**, made with [Ara 3D Gen](https://github.com/olatunde85/3d-gen) (MIT) from MakeHuman's CC0 bodies, skins, hair and clothes. Every outfit in the game became its own character:
  - the player;
  - Maya, Priya, Arjun, Raj, Gail, the Nguyens, Rosa, Marco, Ms Tran, Elias and Sam;
  - the five office staff;
  - four site crew;
  - ten townsfolk.

  Colours, hard hats, vests and caps match the old figures. Faces, hair and proportions are realistic.
- **Motion-captured animation**: 21 clips from the CMU motion capture database, shared by everyone:
  - idle, walk, run;
  - sit, drive, ride a horse, ride a jet ski, steer a boat, fly a helicopter;
  - swim, wave, jump, and a few gestures.

  The clips are scaled to each body's hip height. Walking and running speed up or slow down to match ground speed, so feet don't slide.
- **The wardrobe still works.** The player's model is dressed in white and recoloured per region in the shader: skin, shirt, trousers, shoes, hair, hat and vest. Fabric shading is kept, and the vest disappears when it's off. Hats and hair length switch between seven base models.
- Seated people sit where the seat is. Swimmers lie flat as before.

## How it's built

- **`game/assets-src/catalog.source.json`** records every asset's source, creator, licence and processing steps. **`game/src/assets/catalog.json`** is the generated registry the game reads. Each entry has:
  - id, category, file paths, source URL, creator and licence;
  - real-world size;
  - collision setup;
  - LODs, with distances, triangle counts and file sizes;
  - material variants;
  - animations, the rig, and tint regions.
- **The pipeline** (`game/scripts/assets`, see its README) uses glTF Transform, meshoptimizer, sharp and KTX-Software. For each asset it:
  - normalizes size and facing;
  - simplifies each LOD;
  - packs crowd characters into one texture atlas, so most people are one draw call;
  - writes KTX2 textures and Meshopt-compressed geometry.

  A person is 200–700 KB close up and about 60 KB at a distance. All 41 people and the animations are 18 MB on disk.
- **At runtime** (`game/src/assets`):
  - **Loading**: KTX2 and Meshopt decoding, one shared copy of each file, and a small priority download queue (the player and nearby people first).
  - **LOD and streaming**: LODs are picked by distance, and detailed LODs nobody uses are freed when model memory goes over budget. The budget is 220 MB on integrated GPUs and 420 MB otherwise.
  - **Pooling**: people beyond sight range hand their copy back to a pool for the next person who walks into view.
  - **Instancing**: `AssetInstances` draws many copies of a static model with one draw call per part per LOD. It's ready for houses and parked cars.
- **Adaptive graphics**: "fast" graphics (G, or automatic when the frame rate drops) shorten the LOD distances and how far people are drawn, and animate fewer of them each frame. If it still runs under 30 FPS, the models step down once more to a "minimum" setting, and step back up when there's headroom.
- **Credits**: a Credits link on the title screen. `game/ASSETS.md` is the full inventory and licence record; `game/CREDITS.md` lists the creators.

## Benchmark

Open the game with **`?bench`** to measure the realistic models, or **`?bench&classic`** to measure the old ones. Add `&fast` to measure fast graphics.

The benchmark visits four busy spots, lets each one load, then measures six seconds at each:

- average FPS and 1% lows;
- draw calls and triangles;
- GPU geometries and textures;
- JS heap;
- model data loaded.

The results show on screen and in the console.

My workspace renders in software (no GPU), so its frame rates are about 1 FPS and mean nothing. Please run `?bench` on your laptop for the real numbers. Draw calls and memory don't depend on the GPU:

| Stop (fast graphics) | Draw calls, classic → realistic | Triangles, classic → realistic | JS heap MB | Model data MB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ridge job site | 7,995 → 7,851 | 4.34 M → 4.30 M | 327 → 351 | 0 → 0.7 |
| Downtown Owawa | 1,621 → **448** | 3.73 M → 3.56 M | 344 → 372 | 0 → 1.7 |
| Suburbs | 1,230 → 1,147 | 3.99 M → 4.03 M | 342 → 380 | 0 → 2.6 |
| Vataloo campus | 437 → 340 | 3.73 M → 3.78 M | 339 → 356 | 0 → 2.6 |

Downtown, where most people are, has 72% fewer draw calls. Each old figure was about 40 separate meshes; a realistic person is one to three. The Ridge's 8,000 draw calls are mostly code-built houses and props, which the next phases replace with instanced models.
